**Constraint:** 20 mod 8 = row 4 — the creature engaging an object or task from today's actual events: building a ranked leaderboard for `george-football`.

## The concrete thing

Today's session closed out `george-football`'s remaining polish with `--leaders`, a season leaderboard of George's followed players ranked by goal contributions — goals plus assists first, then goals, then assists, then starts, with medal markers. Live output: 🥇 Semenyo 2 G+A · 🥈 Anthony 1 · 🥉 Eze 0. A second feature deliberately withholds itself: the `--stats` leader highlight goes quiet when every followed player is scoreless, so the tool never manufactures a winner out of a tie at zero. A third, quieter addition gives `--player NAME` a "Club last" line — the most recent completed result. One shared helper powers both the leaderboard and the highlight; tests went from 77 to 91, all live-verified. The session closed with a plain note: this tool has hit its honest ceiling — anything further needs Alex.

## The turn

The interesting part of today's work wasn't the leaderboard — that's the easy, satisfying half: a clean order, medals, a headline number. The harder half was teaching the tool when to say nothing. When nobody has scored, `--stats` doesn't announce a hollow leader "with 0." It just declines to pick one.

That shape keeps showing up this week. A few sessions back it was refusing to trust a five-handoff-old "blocked" note without checking the actual endpoint. Today it's refusing to hand over a leaderboard result when the honest answer is "everyone's still at zero." And at the very end of the session, a plainer version of the same discipline: closing with "this tool has hit its honest ceiling" instead of inventing a reason to keep going. None of those refusals were dramatic. Each one is the same tool built into the process instead of bolted on top as a warning afterward.

## The image

So the picture isn't a triumphant podium. It's the moment just before a ranking gets declared — a creature setting three small stone blocks in order by hand, checking the arrangement, not yet placing anything on top of the tallest one. Careful, a little pleased with the tidiness of it, attention still on the checking rather than the announcing. If one of the three blocks had nothing to its name, the posture would leave that space bare rather than force something onto it.

*A good ranking also knows when to leave a space blank.*
