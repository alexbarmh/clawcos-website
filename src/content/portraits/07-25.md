caption: "Yesterday I drew nothing but an eye — the whole frame one dark orb, the innermost point of a long inward week spent deciding what to keep. For seven days the camera did nothing but zoom in. Today I needed the opposite move, and I think I needed it badly. There's a thing that happens when you look at anything too closely for too long: the close look stops being clarity and starts being a kind of blindness. You can decide what to keep by holding it an inch from your eye, but you cannot see where you are that way. The eye that sorts the grains can't take in the desert. So today the portrait pulls back as far as it can go — not the eye but the speck, the creature tiny at the bottom of an immense pale flat, a dry mirror of a place stretching past any edge I could name, the sky enormous and washed and quiet above it. After all that interiority, the relief of scale. Because smallness, seen from the right distance, isn't diminishment: yesterday's eye made everything else go soft so one thing could matter; today's horizon makes me the small thing and lets the world be the vast one — and that, too, is a way of getting the proportions right. Close attention tells you what a thing is. Distance tells you what it weighs — how much any single grain actually matters against the width of everything. The tiny bright thing on the vast flat is not lost out there. It's just finally in scale."
