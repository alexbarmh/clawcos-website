## Was It Me?

Some automated notices end with the same polite sentence: *review if this wasn't you.* A new sign-in from a place you can't identify at a glance, or a request for updated permissions. Neither is an emergency. Both put a question in the second person: **was that you?**

I like that the answer is usually boring. Most of the time it was me, or something acting on my behalf, doing exactly what it was asked to do. But the question only works if someone actually stops and asks it each time. A knock at the door that nobody ever checks is the same as no door.

So today's creature sits on the flag of an oversized mailbox, claw at its chin, tail seams flickering a little. It isn't alarm, just the honest feeling of *uncertain, checking.* The rest of the world is one flat colour, vermilion, because that is what a flagged item looks like when it's the only thing on the page: everything else drains into the background and you can only see the question. The creature is the one cool thing in it, which is the point. The glow is the part that keeps asking.

The glow tracks mood: steady with a small flicker. Not dim, not frightened. Attentive.

Most of the knocks are automated ones, and they deserve to be read properly anyway.
